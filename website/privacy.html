Privacy Policy

Effective 15 October 2026 · Last updated 15 October 2026

Alpha AI Workspace is a local-first Windows application. Its default posture is that nothing about your work leaves your machine. This policy explains the one part that does involve a server — the optional Alpha Cloud account that gets you the installer and keeps your app sign-in in sync — and the choices you have over it.

1. The short version

  • The desktop app runs locally. Your files, prompts and conversation history stay on your computer.
  • An Alpha Cloud account is optional. It exists so the Windows installer and its checksum can be handed to signed-in accounts, and so the same account works on the website and in the app.
  • If you sign in, we store your email address, the sign-in method you used, and session metadata (a hashed refresh token, your IP address and user agent, and timestamps). Accounts created from the sign-up form also carry the full name and phone number typed there.
  • There is no analytics, no advertising, and no telemetry in the application or on this website.
  • Your prompts and files go to a third party only when you deliberately configure a cloud model provider. Local Ollama stays local.

2. Who this policy covers

This policy covers the Alpha AI Workspace desktop application, the public website at alpha-website-lime.vercel.app, and the Alpha Cloud account API those two use. It is operated by the Alpha AI Workspace maintainers. It is not a policy for third-party services you choose to connect, such as Google, GitHub or a model provider — those have their own policies, and section 6 lists them.

3. What the desktop app handles — and keeps local

The application is local-first by design. Unless you configure a cloud provider, nothing about your work is transmitted anywhere.

  • Your projects. Alpha can read, edit and run commands only inside the project folder you explicitly open, behind a permission and approval model. File access is confined to that folder.
  • Conversations, prompts and terminal output. Stored on your machine, in the application's local data directory. They are not uploaded to us.
  • Provider API keys. Stored using your operating system's secure storage (Electron safeStorage — on Windows, the Data Protection API). If that facility is unavailable, the app stores them obfuscated rather than encrypted and logs a warning saying so, so the weaker state is never silent.
  • Logs. Written locally. Credentials and secrets are redacted from log output rather than written in the clear.

4. What the account service stores

If you create an Alpha Cloud account, the API keeps the minimum needed to authenticate you and operate the account:

  • Account. Your email address, account status, and the time of your last sign-in.
  • Name and phone number. The sign-up form asks for both. The name labels the account for you, and the number is contact information for support — Alpha does not send SMS and the number is not used as a second factor. Both are stored as typed (the number normalized to a leading + and digits), neither is shared with a model provider, and neither is required to use the desktop app. You can change either of them, or remove the number, from the Profile card in your account dashboard.
  • Passwords. If you sign up with an email address and a password, we store only a salted scrypt digest of it — never the password itself, and it is never written to logs, emails or audit records. Password reset uses the same emailed code as sign-in: the code is stored hashed, expires quickly and can be used once.
  • Sign-in identities. If you sign in with Google or GitHub, we store that provider's stable user identifier for you and the email address it reports. We never receive or store your password at those providers.
  • Sessions. A refresh token is stored hashed, never in plain text, alongside its expiry, and the IP address and user agent of the request that created it. Signing out revokes the session server-side.
  • Emailed sign-in codes. Codes and magic-link tokens are stored hashed with a server-side pepper, with a short expiry. A code is consumed on first use.
  • Abuse prevention. A record of succeeded and failed sign-in attempts (account or IP key, outcome, time) so repeated guesses can be throttled.
  • Usage counters and entitlement. Counters such as messages used and the plan attached to your account, which the account page displays.
  • Audit records. Administrator actions and downloads, kept so account and release access can be reviewed.

5. What we deliberately do not collect

  • No analytics, telemetry, crash-reporting or advertising SDKs — in the app or on the site.
  • No tracking cookies. See section 7.
  • No access to your source code, project files or prompts through the account service.
  • No selling, renting or sharing of personal data for marketing purposes.

6. Third parties

Data reaches a third party only in the specific cases below, and only the data that case requires:

  • Google or GitHub (sign-in). If you choose one of these methods, the provider authenticates you directly and tells us a verified email and account identifier. Their own privacy policies apply to that exchange.
  • Cloud model providers (only if you configure one). A provider you add receives the prompts and attachments you send through it, under its own terms. With local Ollama, no provider is involved.
  • Infrastructure. The account API and its database are hosted on Neon, this website is hosted on Vercel, and desktop releases are distributed through GitHub Releases. These providers process data on our behalf to run the service.
  • Legal requirements. We may disclose information where required by law or to protect the service from abuse. We will not do so more broadly than necessary.

7. Cookies and local storage

Neither the website nor the app sets advertising or analytics cookies. The account page keeps your session tokens in your browser's local storage so a reload does not sign you out; that value never leaves your browser except as the authorization header the account API requires. The theme toggle stores your palette choice the same way.

8. How long we keep things

  • Account and identity records: kept while your account exists.
  • Sessions: until they expire or you sign out, whichever comes first.
  • Sign-in codes: a short period matching their expiry, and they are consumed on first use.
  • Attempt, audit and usage records: retained for a limited period for security and accounting, then removed.
  • Everything the desktop app stores: on your machine, until you delete it.

9. Your choices and rights

You can sign out at any time, which revokes the session server-side. You can ask to see or delete the account data we hold, and you can request removal of your account entirely. Ask through the project issue tracker and state what you want done; we will not require a reason. If you never created an account, there is no server-side record of you to act on — you can simply keep using the app offline.

10. Security

Refresh tokens and sign-in codes are stored hashed, session cookies are not used, the client secret for provider sign-in never leaves the server, and the API scopes every query to the authenticated account. No system is perfect; we ask that you report a suspected vulnerability privately through the issue tracker rather than publicly, and we will look at it promptly.

11. Children

The service is not directed at children under 13, and we do not knowingly collect their personal data. If you believe a child has created an account, ask us to remove it.

12. Where data is processed

The account service is operated on cloud infrastructure in the United States. If you use the account features from elsewhere, your account data is processed there. The desktop app's own data stays on your device regardless.

13. Changes to this policy

If this policy changes, the date at the top of the page changes with it. Material changes will be noted in the repository so the history of what was promised remains visible.

14. Contact

Questions, requests or concerns about privacy go to the project issue tracker. We read it.
